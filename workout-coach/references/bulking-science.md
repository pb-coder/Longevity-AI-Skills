# Bodyweight changes and gaining phases

A gain phase is a declared intention. The weekly coach compares a target with repeated, comparable weigh-ins from the current phase. Performance, enjoyment, tolerability, and optional waist observations inform the review. A change on the scale reflects body mass, including shifts in water and glycogen.

## Choosing a rate

Use a signed target tied to the person's reference bodyweight and training goals. Larger gains can yield more fat without a clear across-lift strength or muscle-size advantage. A modest target and repeatable training give the coach a clearer basis for review. [Helms et al. surplus trial](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-023-00651-y) · [Garthe et al. gaining study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23679146/)

## Reading the feedback

- Continue supportive training while the trend and performance are consistent with the goal.
- If gain is faster than intended over comparable observations, discuss recent context and other evidence before reducing a surplus.
- If gain is slower than intended, consider training progress, coverage, and the agreed goal before changing intake.
- A lifting plateau can reflect programming, equipment, logging, technique, effort, fatigue, or adherence. Use it to review the lift and program.
- For cutting, use the signed phase target and training feedback. Faster-than-intended loss warrants a review of whether to ease the deficit. [Garthe et al. weight-loss trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21558571/)

## The 7,700 kcal/kg constant

The convention approximates an energy equivalent for a kilogram of tissue. Historical reports use it to express hypothetical intake from a device-derived energy estimate and target weight rate. Tissue composition and expenditure change over time. Device values are estimates, and the observed scale trend alone cannot determine intake or the cause of a change in the basal estimate. Use a configured intake figure as a chosen target. [Hall et al. dynamic energy model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3880593/) · [Shcherbina et al. wearable validation](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4426/7/2/3)
